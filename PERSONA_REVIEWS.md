# INTHEGYM — Ten Simulated User Reviews

**What this is.** Ten invented people, each a plausible INTHEGYM user, reviewing the app as it
stands on `staging` (19 October 2026). They were written to find weaknesses, not to praise.
Every claim they make is grounded in what the code actually does — copy, prices, gates, flows —
but nobody actually used the app: these are informed simulations, not research. Treat them as
hypotheses to test with real users, not as findings.

**Assumptions.** UK users (prices in £). Reviews judge the player experience: the 5-tab bar,
signup → verify email → 4-step account creation → paywall → MyDay. The DISCOVER and PROFILE tabs
are reviewed as if their rollout had shipped.

The synthesis — the themes ranked by how many people raised them — is at the bottom.

## 1. Jordan, 19 — "Nice-looking, but it assumes I already know what I'm doing"

**Who.** First-year uni student, joined the campus gym in September. Watches a lot of gym TikTok,
has never followed a programme. Money is tight: £9.99/month for the gym is already a stretch.

**Sign-up.** "The welcome screen is honestly cool — the little cards animating, and I could try a
workout before signing up, which no other app let me do. The example workout was the best part of
the whole experience. Then sign-up was fine until it made me go to my email. I signed up with my uni
email, which goes to Outlook, which I never open, and the verification sat in 'Other' for ten
minutes. I nearly gave up. Every other app I've got lets me just press 'Sign in with Apple'."

**Subscription.** "Straight after I made my account there was a paywall. I hadn't even seen the app
yet. £2.99 a *week*? That's more than my phone contract per month if you do the maths — £155 a year.
The yearly one is obviously the one they want you to pick and it's £60 upfront, which I don't have.
I closed it. At least the X was right there and it didn't trick me."

**What it offers.** "Logging is free which is fair. But I opened MyDay and it's a blank timeline
saying 'check in → train → rate'. Check in to what? Rate what? RPE means nothing to me. There's a
Getting Started card which helped a bit. What I actually wanted was someone telling me *what to do*
— a beginner plan, or 'here's a push day'. DISCOVER has workouts other people made, but I've got no
idea which are any good for a beginner. Nothing says 'beginner' or 'how long this takes'."

**Ease of use.** "Searching exercises is good. But I couldn't find 'cable crossover' the way my
mate calls it and there's no way to add my own exercise. Logging one set of one exercise took me
four screens."

**Continue?** "Probably not. I'll go back to my Notes app. If there was a free beginner programme
and stats weren't locked I'd stay." **15%**

**Top asks:** Sign in with Apple/Google · a beginner programme or 'what should I do today' ·
custom exercises · a cheaper/student plan or a monthly trial.

## 2. Priya, 34 — "The analytics thinking is excellent. The logging speed isn't there yet."

**Who.** Data analyst, powerlifts four days a week, two years on Strong then Hevy. Moves apps for
better analytics, and leaves them over friction during sets.

**Sign-up.** "Painless enough. I appreciated that height and weight were optional and that the app
said why it wanted them. The username lowercasing itself was a nice touch. Verify-email is a mild
annoyance but it advanced by itself, which is better than most."

**Subscription.** "The paywall is one of the most honest I've seen — real dates for the trial, a
reminder promised, saving computed rather than invented. Credit for that. But the pricing is out of
the market. Hevy Pro is a fraction of £59.99 a year and Strong is about half. I'd need pro to be
clearly *better*, not just present. And the only trial is on yearly. I'd have liked to try STATS on
monthly before committing £60."

**What it offers.** "STATS is the best-thought-out stats tab I've seen in a consumer app. Two
separate ACWRs, explained, instead of one blended number. Weekly bars rather than daily noise.
Someone here knows sports science. **But**: there's no estimated 1RM, no per-lift PR history I can
browse, no RPE-based e1RM, no way to export to CSV. And I can't import my two years from Hevy, so
STATS would be empty for a month. An ACWR that needs 28 days of data shows me nothing on day one."

**Ease of use.** "Inside a workout it's great — tapping the circle on a set pill to complete it at
the prescribed weight is exactly right. But outside a template, logging a single exercise is four
screens per set. There's no plate calculator, no 'previous: 100 × 5' shown beside the set, no
warm-up sets, no supersets. Rest timers only exist if the template set them. And no Apple Watch,
which is how I log on Strong."

**Continue?** "I'll run it next to Hevy for a month. If import and e1RM arrive, I'd switch." **35%**

**Top asks:** import from Strong/Hevy (CSV) · e1RM and PR history · 'last time' values beside each
set · Apple Watch · CSV export · trial on monthly.

## Synthesis — where to improve

Ranked by how many of the ten raised it, then by severity.

### 1. The paywall arrives before the value — 8 of 10
(Jordan, Priya, Dave, Callum, Ellie, Aisha, Ben, Tom indirectly)

The paywall shows straight after account creation, before the user has logged a single set. Dave
thought it was mandatory. Combined with STATS being entirely locked, free users never see the thing
that would make them pay: *their own* numbers.

> **Decision (20 October 2026): the onboarding paywall stays where it is, straight after account
> creation.** Most trial starts happen on the onboarding paywall, on day zero; move it later and
> most people never see it. The complaint is real, but it is the expected cost of that choice.
> The welcome screen's try-before-signup demo and pro previews are where the value gets shown
> *before* the paywall, and the paywall can always be closed. So the fixes below make the early
> paywall land better rather than moving it.

- **Make closing the paywall obvious**, without making the paywall any weaker. Dave read it as
  compulsory. A visible "Not now" / "Continue with free" under Restore, alongside the close
  button, says the app is usable for free.
- **Say on the paywall what stays free** — logging, workouts, DISCOVER, profiles — so closing it
  feels like a choice, not a dead end.
- **Lean harder on the welcome previews.** They are now the only value someone sees before the
  paywall, so the "Try a workout" and "Preview" cards carry more weight.
- **Give free users a taste of their own stats**: this week's sessions, a streak, one lift's trend.
  Keep ACWR, the 12-week chart and PERFORMANCE pro. "See it add up" on the welcome screen currently
  promises something a free user never gets.
- Callum's point is specific and fixable: the fatigue check needs three sessions (weeks) to
  calibrate, and the trial is two weeks. Either extend the trial or let calibration show a first
  reading earlier.

### 2. Pricing — 7 of 10
- **Weekly £2.99 hurts trust** more than it earns (Jordan, Aisha, Ben). Annualised it is £155.
  Consider dropping it, or testing without it.
- **Yearly £59.99 is high against the logging-app market** (Priya, Jordan, Mo) and fair against the
  performance-testing market (Callum, Ellie). The app sells as a logger but is priced as a
  testing tool. Lead with performance in pricing messaging, or split tiers.
- **Trial only on yearly** pushes people who need short commitments (Ellie, Priya) away. Test a
  trial on monthly, or a cheaper short plan.
- No student / family / team pricing.

### 3. Missing integrations — 6 of 10
No Apple Health, no Apple Watch, no Strava, no Sign in with Apple, no import from Strong/Hevy, no
export. Mo's point matters most: **training load that cannot see half the week is wrong, not just
incomplete** — the same honesty principle STATS applies to its two ACWRs. Priority order suggested
by the reviews:
1. Sign in with Apple (signup drop-off, privacy)
2. Apple Health — read workouts and bodyweight, write strength sessions
3. CSV import from Strong / Hevy (removes the "empty for 28 days" problem)
4. Data export (GDPR, trust)
5. Watch app

### 4. Logging friction outside templates — 5 of 10
(Jordan, Priya, Dave, Mo, Aisha)
- Logging a standalone exercise is several screens per set; the workout session's quick-complete
  is the right model and should be the default everywhere.
- **No custom exercises.** If it's not in the catalogue, it can't be logged.
- **Past days are read-only.** Dave's missed Tuesday is a real and common case; an empty day reads
  as a missed session in the week card and streak.
- Show "last time" values beside each set.
- No rest timer outside templates; no supersets, warm-ups, circuits, AMRAP/EMOM.
- The date strip shows only the last month, so free users can't look back further at all.

### 5. Coaching doesn't exist for new users — 3 of 10, highest revenue
(Sarah, Callum, Ellie)
The app has every monitoring metric a coach or physio wants and no way to share it. Sarah would bring
25 paid seats. Even before full coach mode, **a shareable results export (PDF / link)** would serve
Callum's and Ellie's coach and physio. The coach-pass idea in *Future Ideas* is exactly what Sarah
asked for unprompted.

### 6. Jargon and beginner guidance — 4 of 10
(Jordan, Dave, Aisha, Mo)
"Check-in", "RPE", "ACWR", "training load", "readiness" all assume an athlete. There is no programme,
no "what should I do today", no beginner/equipment/duration tags in DISCOVER. Options: plain-English
subtitles under every metric, a "Beginner" curated workout set, and filters for equipment and time.

### 7. Onboarding length and email verification — 4 of 10
(Jordan, Dave, Aisha, Ben)
Email verification before onboarding loses people whose mail is slow or filtered. Username *and*
display name confused Dave. Consider: Sign in with Apple (no verification step), verifying email
later (gate only social features on it), and collapsing account creation to username only, with
the rest offered later.

### 8. Social mechanics are half-built — 2 of 10, but structural
(Tom, Aisha)
- **Clip watching is pro.** That removes the audience for anyone who creates. Gate recording, not
  watching — or allow some free views.
- **Workouts default to public.** Aisha didn't expect it. Default private, or ask once.
- No feed, no push notifications for likes/comments/follows, no share-out with deep links.

### 9. Trust and polish — 2 of 10
(Ben, Dave)
- **`ProPaywallLinks.privacyPolicy` is `nil`.** That is an App Review 3.1.2 rejection, and the first
  thing a cautious user checks.
- Legacy UIKit screens are still broken in dark mode.
- Small grey secondary text; check Dynamic Type support on the main screens.

### 10. Retention hooks — 3 of 10
(Aisha, Jordan, Dave)
Nothing brings people back: no reminders, no weekly summary, no streak nudges. The trial reminder
shows the notification infrastructure is already there. A single opt-in "training reminder"
(asked after the first workout, the same way the trial reminder asks after purchase) is cheap.

### What reviewers praised (keep these)
- The **try-before-signup demo workout** and welcome previews (Jordan, Callum).
- The **paywall's honesty**: real dates, computed savings, promised reminder, always closable
  (Priya, Ben, Ellie).
- **STATS' sports-science rigour** — two honest ACWRs, weekly bars (Priya, Callum).
- **The weekly fatigue check and single-leg / balance tests** — genuinely differentiated (Callum,
  Ellie).
- **Quick-complete set pills** inside a workout (Priya).
- Offline logging, per-user local storage, real account deletion (Ben).

### Work, in order of importance

Ranked by what it costs to leave undone: first what blocks release or breaks trust, then what
loses people in the first week, then what limits growth. Size: **S** under a day, **M** a few days,
**L** a week or more, **XL** a project.

**Tier 1 — blocks release or breaks trust**
1. **Privacy policy URL** (S). `ProPaywallLinks.privacyPolicy` is `nil` and there is no privacy
   policy link anywhere in the app — an App Review 3.1.2 rejection. Needs a hosted policy first.
   Add it to Settings → About as well.
2. **Workouts private by default** (S). `WorkoutBuilderManager.isPublic` defaults to `true` and
   `reset()` restores it, so every new user publishes to DISCOVER without choosing to.
3. **Paywall: "Continue with free" + what stays free** (S). The paywall stays early (decided). It
   must not read as compulsory.
4. **Pricing decisions** (S in code, mostly App Store Connect): drop or test the weekly plan, a
   trial on monthly, and a trial long enough for the three-check fatigue calibration.

**Tier 2 — loses people in the first week**
5. **Custom exercises** (M). If it's not in the catalogue, it cannot be logged at all.
6. **Logging a past day** (M–L). A forgotten session reads as a missed one in the week card and
   streak. Touches logging, raw logs and `DailyTotal`s — scope it first.
7. **A free "this week" view of the user's own training** (M). Sessions, streak, one lift's trend.
   Makes "See it add up" true for free users and is the best advert for STATS.
8. **Sign in with Apple** (M). Cuts signup drop-off, skips email verification, private relay email.
9. **Faster standalone logging** (M–L). Quick-complete outside templates, "last time" values beside
   each set, a rest timer outside templates.
10. **Training reminders** (S–M). Opt-in, asked after the first workout, the way the trial reminder
    is asked after purchase. Nothing currently brings anyone back.
11. **Plain-English labels and beginner guidance** (M). Subtitles for RPE / check-in / ACWR /
    readiness; beginner, equipment and duration filters on DISCOVER.

**Tier 3 — widens who the app is for**
12. **Apple Health** (L). Read workouts and bodyweight, write strength sessions. Training load that
    can't see half the week is wrong, not just incomplete.
13. **Data export** (M). GDPR access and portability, and what cautious users check before
    committing.
14. **Import from Strong / Hevy** (M–L). Removes the "STATS empty for 28 days" problem for switchers.
15. **Clip watching free** (S in code, a business decision). Gate recording, not watching.
16. **Shorter onboarding** (M). Username only up front; profile and body details offered later.
